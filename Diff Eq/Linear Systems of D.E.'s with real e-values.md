$x'=Ax$
where $A$ is a $2 \times 2$ with constant entries
We'll look for solutions of the form $e^{\lambda t}v$

If the guess works, and $v\neq \vec{0}$ then $v$ is an eigne vector of $A$ with an e-value of $\lambda$
We look for solutions of the form
$x=e^{\lambda t}v$

Substituting
$\lambda e^{\lambda t}v=e^{\lambda t}Av$
$\lambda v = Av$

Theorem: For $x'=Ax$ ($2 \times 2$ case), if $A$ has constant coefficients, and e-values $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ and $x_1=e^{\lambda_1 t}v_1,x_2=e^{\lambda_2 t}v_2$
where $v_1,v_2$ are corresponding e-vectors, then $x_1,x_2$ is a basis of solutions PROVIDED $\lambda_1\neq\lambda_2$ if $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$, then there may be only 1 linearly independent eigenvector
