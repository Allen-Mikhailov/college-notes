If $A(t)$ is $n \times n$, then $\mathbb{S}$ is n-dimensional. $\mathbb{S}$ is the solution space
- There is a basis $\vec{x_1},...,\vec{x_n}$ of $\mathbb{S}$


### Example 1:
$$
\vec{x_1}=
\left[
\begin{array}{c}
0 \\ 0 \\ e^{3t}
\end{array}
\right],
\vec{x_2}=
\left[
\begin{array}{c}
2e^{2t} \\ e^{2t} \\ e^{2t}
\end{array}
\right],
\vec{x_3}=
\left[
\begin{array}{c}
e^t \\ e^t \\ 0
\end{array}
\right]
$$

$\vec{x_1},\vec{x_2},\vec{x_3} \in \mathbb{S}$
To show that they are linearly independent we just have to show that they are linearly independent at **one** time
